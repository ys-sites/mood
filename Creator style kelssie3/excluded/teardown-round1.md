## The format in one block

This is built from the four talking-to-camera winners (#1, #3, #4, #5). It is what someone
would film and edit.

**Script**
- Runtime: 15–46s (37, 15, 46, 44)
- Time to hook: 0.0s in all four. No intro, no breath, first word on frame 1.
- Hook: one statement sentence, 12–21 words, 3.2–6.2s. It is a claim about her or
  about "your" agent/home, never a question. ("Before you hire a realtor, don't just
  take their word for it." / "Let me be the first to say your property deserves the
  investment from your real estate agent.")
- Body: 3–5 beats, **one sentence each**, 4–10s each. Every beat carries one proof:
  a number (27 families, $10 million, top 1%), a platform (Zillow Showcase,
  Homes.com), reviews, a map.
- Close: one or two sentences of thanks or a CTA, 20–25 words, starting at 68–87%
  of runtime. In 3 of 4 it is the **fastest** part of the video (280–310 WPM).
- WPM: 191–235 overall. Longest pause in any winner: 0.62s.

**Edit (the part you asked about)**
1. **Cinematic look.** Shallow depth of field, natural light, a styled luxury home
   (interior or terrace). She is framed wide: full body or three-quarter, not a tight
   head-and-shoulders.
2. **One setup per sentence.** 3–6 different rooms or angles, same outfit. She cuts in
   the gap between sentences: 15 of 22 cuts sit in a sentence gap and 4 more on a comma
   pause. 15 of the 20 sentence gaps in the three multi-setup videos have a cut.
3. **Punch in on the same take** for a phrase, then back out. These were measured at
   1.15–1.6× scale (#4 at 29.6s in, 38.1s out; #5 at 32.6s and 40.7s).
4. **Running captions: tiny and quiet.** 1–2 words, thin white sans (closest Google font: Inter
   300), cap height about 2.2% of frame, centred at 49% height, which is on her chest. No
   highlight colour, no box, no outline.
5. **One hero keyword per sentence.** These are huge (TOP 1%, BUT, average, LAST YEAR,
   HOUSE). They sit in the upper third and are **matted behind her head**: her hair covers
   part of the letters. A small thin lead-in word sits above the keyword ("than the",
   "and every", "my") and a small bold follow-on phrase sits below it ("agent",
   "serve others"). The colour is picked per video: purple, slate blue, lavender, coral,
   red script, teal italic. Each one mixes a heavy grotesk with an italic serif or script.
6. **Proof cards on the naming word.** Real artefacts float in at hand or waist height:
   client photos in a fanned 3D carousel, magazine covers, a map, platform logos,
   review screenshots, her Instagram profile with a cursor, her own past videos, and
   comment screenshots. On the 2-frames-a-second sheets every card I checked was up
   within about 0.3s of the word naming it.
7. **Density.** 13–16 hero words and cards in the first 32–40s of each
   multi-setup winner, so something new appears every 2–3s. The captions run underneath all of it.
8. **Silence cut tight.** The max pause kept is 0.5s.

The machine-readable version is in `style.json` (pace, zoom, captions).

## The shared formula

| Beat | What every talking winner does |
|---|---|
| Hook | First word at 0.0s. One statement sentence, 12–21 words, about the viewer's decision or her results. The hero keyword is behind her head on frame 1. |
| Body | 3–5 one-sentence beats, 4–10s each, a new setup or punch at each sentence break. |
| Per beat | One claim, one proof card on the naming word (±0.3s), one hero keyword, and 1–2-word captions running underneath. |
| Close | Thanks or CTA, 20–25 words, 68–87% in. Usually the fastest part (280–310 WPM). A CTA is acted out on screen when there is one (#3). |

## Verdict

**Worth learning, mainly as an editing system.** The script format is a fairly standard
claim → proof → close. What sets her apart is the layered edit:
1. a new setup on every sentence
2. tiny running captions
3. a huge keyword behind her head
4. a real proof card landing on the word that names it

All of it is dense enough that something changes every 2–3 seconds.

**Overlap:** the style-edit skill already does captions, zooms, pace and word-timed
visuals, and `style.json` now carries her measured pace, zoom and caption look. Two
things are new and not in `style.json`:
- **Text behind the subject.** This needs a person matte (segmentation) per frame.
- **Two-tier kinetic type.** A small lead-in word, a giant keyword, then a bold
  follow-on phrase, with mixed fonts.

Both are worth asking for when you style-edit your own video.

**Not done yet:** a frame-accurate `events.json` (card in, settle and out to the frame at
30 fps). The timings above come from 2-frames-a-second sheets (±0.25s). That is enough to
learn from, but `events.json` is needed before planning your own video in her style. It
gets built in the edit-plan step.
