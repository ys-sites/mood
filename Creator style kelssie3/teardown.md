# Teardown: @kelssie3 (talking-to-camera only)

Six talking-to-camera videos, pulled 2026-10-04 over two rounds of hand-picked links. The creator's
median is **50,750 views** across their last 40 TikToks. Four videos sit well above it
(1.85× to 8.82×). Two sit at or below it (1.15× and 0.55×) and serve as the control group.
All six were transcribed with Whisper (free). Whisper drops some "um"s and false starts, so the filler counts are
a floor.

Source: https://www.tiktok.com/@kelssie3 · Pulled with the creator-teardown skill

**What counts as talking-to-camera.** A video is kept only if speech covers at least
90% of the runtime with no silent B-roll stretch of 3s or more. The six kept
videos are at 94–100%. The eight set aside are at 72% or less, or are a montage
or lip-sync. Those eight are listed in `excluded/README.txt` and their files are in `excluded/`.
They range from 40,500 to 451,600 views, so this format is one of two that work for her, not
the only one. The listing tours (hook to camera, then drone and voiceover) are the other.

The round-1 teardown, which mixed in three non-talking videos, is kept at
`excluded/teardown-round1.md`. Two of its conclusions are corrected below.

## The format in one block

This is built from all six videos. Where the four winners differ from the two controls, it says so.

**Script**
- Runtime: 15–65.5s. The four winners run 15–46s.
- Time to first word: 0.0s in all six. No intro beat and no breath.
- Hook: one statement sentence, 12–21 words, 3.2–6.2s. In the winners it is a claim
  about her or about "your" agent or home ("Before you hire a realtor, don't just take
  their word for it."). The 27.9K control opens with "Welcome! I'm Kelsey Blevins" instead.
- Body: 3–6 beats of one sentence each, 4–13s each. Each beat makes one claim.
- Close: one or two sentences of thanks, a CTA or a command, starting at 68–89% of
  runtime. It is usually the fastest part (235–310 WPM in 5 of 6).
- WPM: 186–235 per video. The median of 208 is in `style.json`.
- Longest pause in any video: 0.24–0.62s. The 0.44s max-pause figure is in `style.json`.

**Edit:** every one of the six uses this system, including the two controls.
1. **Cinematic look.** Shallow depth of field, natural light and a styled luxury home. She is framed wide,
   full body or three-quarter.
2. **One setup per sentence.** Rooms or angles change, and the outfit stays the same. Across the five multi-setup
   videos, **30 of 37 sentence gaps have a cut (81%)**. #2 is a single take.
3. **Punch in on the same take** for a phrase, then back out. The median zoom is a 1.09× push
   lasting 1.0s, 12.1 a minute. Punches of 1.15–1.6× happen on emphasis lines.
4. **Running captions: tiny and quiet.** 1–2 words, thin white sans (closest Google font: Inter
   300), cap height about 2.2% of frame, centred at 46–50% height, which is on her chest. No highlight,
   no box, no outline.
5. **One hero keyword per sentence, behind her head.** It is huge and in the upper third, matted
   so her hair covers part of the letters. A small thin lead-in sits above ("than the", "your",
   "that most") and a small bold follow-on sits below ("agent", "opportunity"). Colours
   change per video. Heavy grotesk caps are mixed with an italic serif or script.
6. **Proof cards and graphics on the naming word.** These are real artefacts: client photos in a 3D
   carousel, reviews, platform logos, a map, an Instagram profile, an analytics screen, listing
   posts, a ranking badge. The non-proof video (#5) uses metaphor graphics instead: a bar that
   fills on "expanding" and drains on "shrinking", and a strike-through on "you're not". On the
   2-frames-a-second sheets they land between 0.5s before and 0.3s after the word, mostly within ±0.3s.
7. **Density.** 13–25 hero words and cards in the first 32–60s of every video, so something new appears
   every 2–3s.
8. **Silence cut tight.** No pause longer than 0.62s in any of the six.

The machine-readable version is in `style.json`: pace and zoom measured over these six, and captions read
off the sheets.

## Control group: what is habit and what might be the lever

The four winners (#1–#4) run 93,800–447,600 views. The two controls (#5 and #6) run 27,900–58,500.

**Present in the winners AND the controls (habit, not lever):**
- The whole edit system: a setup per sentence (30 of 37 gaps across 5 videos), the hero
  keyword behind her head, tiny chest captions, and an event every 2–3s. All six.
- Proof cards on the naming word: 3 of 4 winners, and #6 has the most of any video.
  **This corrects round 1**, which called proof cards a candidate lever.
- First word at 0.0s, 186–235 WPM, pauses under 0.62s, and a fast close. All six.

**Present ONLY in the winners (candidate levers, each backed by just one control):**

- Built from one frame with a few proofs (a recap, a warning, a mission, a thesis), not

## Voice profile

Measured across all six (838 words, 46 sentences).

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Median sentence length | 18 words (range 1–40). That is long for short-form, which usually runs 6–14. |
| you : I : we | 34 : 40 : 1 (4.1 vs 4.8 per 100 words). #5 is the outlier, at 12 : 0. |
| Imperatives per 100 words | about 1.2 ("don't just take", "look at", "click" ×2, "scroll down", "let's talk", "let me", "just know", "stop waiting", "start saying yes") |
| Filler rate | 0–2.2 per 100 words (Whisper, so a floor) |
| Recurring phrases | "God" (6, in 3 videos) · "serve" (5, in 3) · "every single" (4, in 4) · "for my clients" (3) · "show up" (3) · "honored / humbled / blessed" (3) · "most agents / average agent" (2) · "you and your family" (2) · "click the link in my bio" (2) |
| Register | Warm, polished, sincere and first-person. It sounds scripted, delivered at 200+ WPM with almost no pauses. |

**Moves to check against your own voice** (the script found no hype words):
- Faith references: 6. "God has blessed me with this platform" (#3).
- Credential claims: 6. "I've sold over 40 million dollars in real estate just two years into my
  career" (#6). "Being in the top 1 % of agents in North Carolina" (#4).
- Humility-brag: 3. "looking back at where God has taken me since has humbled me" (#1).

  for my clients" (#3).

- Filler openers and closers: "Let me be the first to say" (#4), "Welcome! I'm Kelsey Blevins" (#6),

**Register call:** in your mouth, this sounds like a well-made brand video for a service
business. The structure transfers cleanly: one claim per sentence, something on screen for each one,
and a fast close. Three things have to change.
- Her sentences average 18 words. That works because every sentence gets a new setup and a
  hero word. If you film in one spot, cut them to 10–14.
- Her proof is real artefacts. Use your own (screenshots, results, client wins), never
  stand-ins. If you have no proof for a beat, use a metaphor graphic like #5's bar.
- Swap the faith and gratitude language for what you actually stand for.

## The shared formula

| Beat | What the four winners do (the controls match on everything except where noted) |
|---|---|
| Hook | First word at 0.0s. One statement sentence, 12–21 words, a claim aimed at the viewer's decision or her results. The hero keyword is behind her head on frame 1. #6 swaps in a greeting and name. |
| Body | 3–5 one-sentence beats, 4–10s each, with a new setup or punch at each sentence break. Total runtime 15–46s (#6: 65.5s). |
| Per beat | One claim, one proof card or metaphor graphic on the naming word (mostly ±0.3s), one hero keyword, and 1–2-word captions running underneath. |
| Close | Thanks, a CTA or a mission line, 20–38 words, 68–87% in. Usually the fastest part. A CTA is acted out on screen when there is one (#2). |

## Verdict

**Worth learning, mainly as an editing system.** All six talking videos, winners and controls,
use the same layered edit:
1. a new setup on every sentence
2. tiny running captions
3. a huge keyword behind her head
4. a proof card or metaphor graphic on the word that names it

All of it is dense enough that something changes every 2–3 seconds. That makes it a reliable recipe
for how her videos look. What it is not is the reason some of them do better than others. The likely
reasons sit in the script: speak to the client, open on the claim, and stay under about 46s.

**Overlap:** the style-edit skill already does captions, zooms, pace and word-timed visuals,
and `style.json` carries her measured numbers from these six videos. Two things are not in `style.json`
and are worth asking for by name:
- **Text behind the subject.** This needs a person matte per frame.
- **Two-tier kinetic type.** A small lead-in, a giant keyword, then a bold follow-on, in mixed fonts.

**Not done yet:** a frame-accurate `events.json`. The timings above come from 2-frames-a-second sheets
(±0.25s). It gets built in the edit-plan step when you hand over your own video.
